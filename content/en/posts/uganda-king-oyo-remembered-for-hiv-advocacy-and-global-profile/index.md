King Oyo Nyimba, who became Uganda's monarch at just three years old, has been remembered for his public role, HIV advocacy and efforts to promote the heritage of the Tooro Kingdom.

Oyo took the throne in 1995 after a succession dispute and remained one of the world's most recognisable traditional rulers for decades.

Beyond his ceremonial duties, he served as a United Nations goodwill ambassador supporting the fight against HIV and AIDS.

His sister, Ruth Komuntale, described him during a church service before his burial as a reserved but cheerful person who maintained his youthful spirit despite the responsibilities of kingship.

Oyo also developed a close relationship with former Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi, whom he appointed as a special adviser. Gaddafi contributed funds toward the renovation of the royal palace.

Oyo died at the age of 34 in the United States while receiving treatment for cancer. He was buried on Saturday september 12, 2026 at the Karambi Royal Tombs in Fort Portal, in Uganda where thousands gathered to pay their final respects.
